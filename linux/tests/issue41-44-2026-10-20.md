# #41–#44: follow-ups of the #5 checks (2026-10-20)

The #5 checks (`issue5-smp-2026-10-19.md`) left four problems, none of them
specific to two CPUs alone: hush ignored `set -e` after a skipped `&&` /
`||` list (#41); the end of ttyGS1 output waited half a second and a kernel
message lost a character under load (#42); pings waited up to 0.9 s, and
some were lost, while `/boot` was written to flash (#43); and a signal
delivered to a handler cost more on two CPUs than on one (#44).  This
record covers the fixes and the checks on the board.

Board: M5Stamp ESP32-P4 + C6 (SDIO), P4 USB Serial/JTAG on COM10.  Only the
P4 flash was written, with `flash-candidate.ps1 -PreserveBoot`; the C6 was
not touched.  No SSID, PSK, password or address is quoted here.

## #41: `set -e` in hush

### Fix (61a4b0a)

`linux/m2/busybox-patches/0001-hush-set-e-fixes.patch`, staged for every
network image, fixes three bugs in how hush's `run_list()` keeps `set -e`
off (details in `linux/m2/busybox-patches/README.md`):

1. A skipped pipe that `&&` or `||` followed left `G.errexit_depth` raised:
   `set -e; false && : || :; false; echo LEAK` printed `LEAK`, and at the
   top level of a script `set -e` stayed off to the end (the issue).
2. The condition of `while` and `until` was not exempt: a loop that ended
   because its condition became false ended the script with status 1.
3. `! pipe` was not exempt: `set -e; ! true` exited.

`S40network` no longer uses `set -e`.  On a running board its top-level
`[ -p "$NET_TICK_FIFO" ] || mkfifo … || true` had turned it off for `apply`,
`reload`, `restart` and `stop`, as the issue suspected.  At boot it was in
effect, and bug 2 ended a station wait that timed out (wrong SSID or PSK)
before it logged `did not associate`.  Each step handles its own failure
instead.  `S05easystick-tmpfs`, `S85easystick-ssh`, `easystick-firstboot` and
`easystick-sdio-diag` keep `set -e`; the patch does not change what they do
(the README has the audit).

### Results

- Host: on an x86 build of the board's BusyBox configuration, 49 test
  scripts print the same as dash and `bash --posix` with the patch; 17
  differed without it.  BusyBox's `shell/hush_test` gives 361 ok and 18
  failed with and without the patch, the same 18.
- `shtest.sh`, the #5 script with 10 new `set -e` cases: 100 of 100 on both
  builds (15.0 s on fix43).  The new cases are the issue's leaks (after a
  skipped `&& … ||` and `|| … ||`, `set +e; set -e` after a skip, a skip in
  a function, a skip and `break`), loops ended by their `while` or `until`
  condition (also `while read` from a file), `! true`, and `false` in a
  loop body, which must still stop the script.
- `bx5.sh` as root, both builds: `set -e; false && : || :; false; echo LEAK`
  prints nothing and exits with 1; a counting `while` loop under `set -e`
  runs to its end (`B2`, status 0); `set -e; ! true; echo A` prints `A`;
  the shape of S40network's station wait prints `appeared
  did-not-associate end`, status 0.  The unpatched `/bin/sh` printed
  `LEAK`, exited with 1 at the end of the loop and exited after `! true`.
- `S40network start` from a copy whose `config.txt` asks for an SSID that
  does not exist (fix41-44, root on the console), first as shipped and then
  with `set -e` added at the top: both waited for the station, logged `did
  not associate (SSID_NOT_FOUND)` after about 65 s and exited with 0.
  `S40network reload` then brought the network back.
- Live drive edits (fix43): `config.txt` was changed in the live drive image
  on the board (a loop mount of `/tmp/boot.img` standing in for the host),
  as root on the console, while the host pinged the board once a second.

| Edit of `config.txt` | bootsync and S40network |
|---|---|
| `hostname=` changed | `Configuration changed: hostname; applying`; the new name was in effect when checked 17 s later, with no network restart |
| `sdio_mhz=5` added | `ES_CONFIG: sdio_mhz= changed; applies at the next boot` |
| an unknown key `zz41=1` added | `ES_CONFIG: zz41 has no targeted apply; restarting the network` |

Every save went `boot commit: staged` → `written to flash` → `[bootsync]
Saved to flash` with no reboot; the last three were saved 5 s after the
edit.  245 pings were sent: the 25 without a reply fell in the two network
restarts (gaps of 24 s and 5 s between replies), and the slowest reply took
986 ms.  SSH as `m5` worked afterwards.

## #42: ttyGS1 console

### Fix (a1eb1f3, 0082)

- `esp32s3_acm_transmit_buffer()` arms the IN-empty interrupt whenever it
  leaves data in `xmit`, also when the IN endpoint is still busy.  Before,
  the rest waited until the serial core's runtime PM (500 ms autosuspend)
  called `start_tx()` again.
- Console output is committed once per write instead of once per
  character.  The wait for FIFO room is counted from the last byte the FIFO
  took and ends after 50 ms (0062: 2 ms per character, then the character
  was dropped), so a host that stops reading costs one wait, not one per
  character.  Characters dropped after that are counted in
  `/sys/class/tty/ttyGS1/console_dropped`.

### Results

The #5 test (`ctest.sh`): a writer sends a burst of 80 lines to ttyGS1 every
second for 18 s while a loop writes KERN_ERR lines to `/dev/kmsg` as fast as
it can; the host logs the port with timestamps.

| | #5 (smp-def) | fix41-44 | fix43 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burst lines received | | 1,360 of 1,360 (17 bursts) | 1,280 of 1,280 (16 bursts) |
| Kernel lines received | | 5,231 of 5,231 | 5,133 of 5,133 |
| Last line of a burst after the one before it | 0.5–0.6 s | median 0, max 1 ms | median 0, max 1 ms |
| Lost characters | 1 | none | none |

A kernel line printed inside a burst line 28 times on fix41-44 and 27 times

## #43: Wi-Fi while `/boot` is written to flash

### Cause

0072 makes each ROM flash read, erase and program under `stop_machine()`:
CPU0 makes the call with interrupts off while CPU1 spins with interrupts
off.  Nothing else runs meanwhile, neither the SDIO host's interrupt nor
the esp-hosted threads that carry Wi-Fi frames.  Each call is short (an
erase about 18 ms, a page program or a sector read much less), but a commit
made about 1,350 of them back to back: it read both slots to find the
active one, read the active slot again, then erased, programmed and read
back all 64 sectors of the inactive slot.  That held the CPUs for nearly all
of the 1.9 s, and the network ran only in the gaps between calls.

UDP probes from the host every 20 ms to an echo server on the board
(MicroPython, which stamps each reply with its uptime) show it (u1 below,
fix41-44, the six commits of the ping run):

- 86 of 5,500 probes were lost, all sent during the commits (2 to 28 of
  about 100 per commit).
- Probes sent during a commit got their reply after a median of 1.44 s
  (p90 2.7 s, max 3.5 s), against 21 ms outside.  Most of the extra time
  came after the echo server had answered (median 1.18 s, against 0.26 s
  on the way to it): the replies waited on the board until the commit let
  the transmit path run.

### Fix (0ea1815)

- The commit compares each sector of the inactive slot with the new image
  and erases and programs only those that differ.  The inactive slot
  usually holds the generation before the active one, and an edit changes
  a few sectors.
- It reads the older record's slot only when the newer record is not
  valid, and takes the active slot's CRC from the record it checked.
- After every 8 ms of ROM calls (`FLASH_BURST_US`) it sleeps for as long as
  they took, so the CPUs are free at least half of the time and no stop is
  longer than one call.
- The kernel log line `boot update committed: … sectors=N/64 rom=…ms
  total=…ms` gives the sectors written, the time in ROM calls and the total.

## #44: handled signals on two CPUs

### Fix (36d23e5, 0083)

Without a vDSO, the NOMMU `setup_rt_frame()` copied the two instructions
that call `rt_sigreturn` into the signal frame and called
`flush_icache_range()`, which is `flush_icache_all()`: with two CPUs online,
an IPI to the other CPU and a wait for it on every handled signal.  0083
points `ra` at the kernel's own copy, `__user_rt_sigreturn` (`li a7,
__NR_rt_sigreturn; ecall` in `.rodata`).  User code runs in M-mode (0031)
from the same PSRAM window as the kernel image, so it can run that copy on
either CPU, and neither the copy nor the flush is needed.  The frame layout
does not change, and an unwinder that finds a signal frame by those two
instructions at `ra`, as libgcc's does, sees the same bytes.

## Review (Devin)

- 0072 (0ea1815): no defect.  A sector is skipped only when the bytes read
  from the inactive slot equal the image, so a half-written slot from an
  interrupted commit is rewritten where it differs, and the metadata is
  still written only after the whole slot reads back with the right CRC.
  The record's CRC is used only after the slot it names has been checked
  against it.  The pacing counters change only under `bootflash_lock`, and
  `fsleep()` runs in process context.  Info: up to 8 ms of busy time can
  carry over into the next commit's first burst.
- 0083 (36d23e5): no defect.  Nothing reads the frame's `sigreturn_code`
  any more; uClibc-ng does not use `SA_RESTORER` on RISC-V; libgcc's
  unwinder matches the two instructions at the return address, which the
  kernel's copy has.
- 0082 (a1eb1f3): no defect.  The IN-empty interrupt is armed under
  `port->lock` in both callers, and it fires only when the host empties the
  IN FIFO, so a host that never reads cannot cause a storm.  Info: console
  output does not take `port->lock`, so it can still interleave with the
  interrupt's output byte by byte, as before.
- hush (61a4b0a): the depth is restored on every way out of `run_list()`
  (`break`, `continue`, `return`, SIGINT).  One finding (low) did not hold:
  that only the last pipe of a condition such as `while a; b; do` is exempt.
  hush's parser gives every pipe of a condition the `if`, `elif`, `while`
  or `until` reserved word, and 11 more host cases with such conditions
  (`hushx5.sh`) print the same as dash and `bash --posix`; the unpatched
  build stopped in the three `while`/`until` cases.

## Open

- The 1.1 s hold-up in one commit of u3 (see #43).  Candidates are the
  esp-hosted transmit retry loop, slowed by the stops, and the C6 side;
  nothing in the kernel log points at either.
- On this NOMMU system a `( … )` or `$(…)` subshell does not inherit `set
  -e` (hush re-executes itself and passes no options).  That is documented
  in `linux/m2/busybox-patches/README.md`, not changed.
- The hush fix is not reported upstream; BusyBox master has the same code.
